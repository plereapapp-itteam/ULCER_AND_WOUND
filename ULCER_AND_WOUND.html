Minor Burns & Referral Criteria

TOS domain · PLE Ulcer & Wound Management

▪What Counts as Minor

  • DMinor burn (managed in the community): superficial (painful, moist, brisk capillary return) and <10% TBSA in adults or <5% TBSA in children, not meeting any referral criterion.
  • Infected burns or burns not healing as expected may need specialist care — phone the burns centre to agree dressings for transfer.

▪Refer to a Specialist Burns Centre

CategoryCriteria (ANZBA)
Size>10% TBSA adults; >5% TBSA children; full thickness >5% TBSA
SiteFace, hands, feet, genitals, perineum, major joints; circumferential limb or chest
MechanismInhalation injury, electrical, chemical, with major trauma
PatientYoung children and the elderly, pregnancy, pre-existing illness
IntentIntentional — assault or self-inflicted
Board trap: a small burn is still a referral if it is on the hand, face, feet, genitals, perineum or over a major joint, or is electrical or chemical, or the patient is pregnant.

▪Causes and Prevention

  • Adults: flame commonest (camp fires, accelerants on barbecues) — mostly young men.
  • PChildren: scalds commonest → turn saucepan handles in, move climbable objects from the stove, keep hot items out of reach.
  • Always ask how it happened: consider self-harm, neglect or abuse.

Assessing Size & Depth

TOS domain · PLE Ulcer & Wound Management

▪Estimating TBSA

ToolUse
Wallace Rule of NinesAdults and children over 10 years (e.g. one arm 9%)
Modified Lund and BrowderChildren under 10 years (head larger proportion)
Palm + fingers~1% TBSA at any age — handy for scattered burns

▪Burn Depth

DepthAppearance and sensationHealing
Epidermal (e.g. sunburn)Pink/red, painful, epidermis intact10–12 days, no scar
Superficial dermal (scald, flash)Pink, moist, blistered, blanches, painful7–21 days; scar unlikely
Mid dermalDark pink/red, sluggish capillary return, less painful14–21 days; scar if >3 weeks; consider referral
Deep dermalMottled pink or white, slow to non-blanching, pressure rather than pain>3 weeks; expect scarring; consider referral
Full thicknessWhite, black, leathery, dry, translucent; no sensation, no blanching>3 weeks; scarring; most need referral
Pain is a good sign: painful, moist and blanching = superficial; painless and non-blanching = deep. Full thickness >1 cm needs surgery.
  • Deroofing blisters helps you judge depth.

First Aid & Initial Care

TOS domain · PLE Ulcer & Wound Management

▪Cooling

  • DCool running water (ideally 15 °C) for 20 minutes — still effective up to 3 hours after the burn; prevents conversion to a deeper burn.
  • Check hose/tap water temperature first. If water is limited: cool water spray, cool wet cloth, or hydrogel (e.g. Burnaid) in adults.
Never apply ice — it causes further injury. Watch for hypothermia during cooling (especially children): cover unburnt areas and keep warm.
  • Cut away stuck or constricting clothing; remove jewellery from the burnt area.

▪Initial Wound Care

  • Adequate analgesia and hydration.
  • Clean with potable water, 0.1% aqueous chlorhexidine or saline; remove foreign material and loose/nonviable skin.
  • Remove hair within 2 cm of the burn.
  • PCheck tetanus status (burns are tetanus-prone wounds).
  • Limb burns: elevate and gentle support/compression for oedema.

▪Deroofing Blisters

  • Why: assess depth, remove nonviable tissue, relieve pain of tense blisters.
  • DHow: consent, analgesia, photo before and after; moist gauze rubbed across thin-walled blisters; sterile forceps and scissors for thick-walled; then dress.

Dressings, Review & Follow-up

TOS domain · PLE Ulcer & Wound Management

▪Dressing by Depth

DepthTreatment
EpidermalCool showers/compress, fluids, moisturise; hydrogel if painful; no secondary dressing needed
Superficial dermalDeroof and debride; heavy exudate first 72 h → silicone foam; silver dressing if contaminated (Acticoat, AquacelAg); minimal exudate → clear acrylic moisture-retention dressing
Mid / deep dermalSilver dressings; debride; consider burns referral (may need graft)
Full thicknessResuscitation and supportive care if needed; most referred; small ones cleansed and debrided with burns-centre advice
Silver sulfadiazine cream is not recommended — may delay healing, works only ~8 h, and hides the wound for reassessment. Use only if silver dressings are unavailable.

▪Review

  • DReview within 24–48 hours. Offer emotional support to patient and family.
  • Not healing as expected (e.g. by 14 days) → wound specialist or burns service.
  • PNo antibiotics unless there is clinical infection.

⚖️Classic Board Distractor Pairs — All Topics

🔥 Referral
  • Adult >10% or child >5% TBSA = refer; smaller superficial = community.
  • Hands, face, feet, genitals, joints, circumferential = refer at any size.
  • Electrical, chemical, inhalation, pregnancy, extremes of age = refer.
📐 Assessment
  • Over 10 = rule of nines; under 10 = Lund and Browder; palm = 1%.
  • Painful, moist, blanching = superficial; painless, non-blanching = deep.
💧 First aid
  • Running water 15 °C for 20 min, useful up to 3 h; no ice.
  • Keep warm while cooling; remove jewellery; check tetanus.
  • Thin blister = moist gauze; thick = forceps and scissors.
🩹 Dressings
  • Sunburn = moisturise ± hydrogel; contaminated = silver dressing.
  • Silver sulfadiazine only if no silver dressing; antibiotics only if infected.
  • Review 24–48 h; not healed by 14 days = refer.

🏁Final 12-Item PLE Mental Checklist

  • Ask how the burn happened; consider abuse, neglect or self-harm.
  • Phone the burns centre before transfer to agree dressings.
  • Cool for a full 20 minutes with running water.
  • Do not use ice.
  • Monitor for hypothermia, especially in children.
  • Cut away constricting clothing and remove jewellery.
  • Give analgesia and ensure hydration.
  • Clean with water, saline or 0.1% chlorhexidine.
  • Deroof blisters to assess depth.
  • Remove hair within 2 cm of the burn.
  • Elevate burnt limbs to limit oedema.
  • Photograph the wound before and after deroofing.
